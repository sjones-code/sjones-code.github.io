ABS braking simulation

A block-diagram model of the anti-lock braking system in a car, built in MATLAB with two teammates (Josh Chung and KC Hinz) for ME 3017 in Spring 2025. It compares a full nonlinear model against a linearized one, and tests three kinds of brake pedal input as the road turns from pavement to ice.

How the model works

  1. Driver input. The brake pedal angle goes up to 15 degrees. Braking starts at 10 s and uses a ramp, a step, or a cosine wave.
  2. Pedal assembly. The pedal turns into brake fluid pressure. The nonlinear model uses sin θ, and the linearized model uses the small-angle form θ.
  3. Tire block. The friction the road can supply is Ff = m g μ, where μ drops from pavement to ice halfway through.
  4. ABS module. It compares brake force to available friction. If the brakes are weaker than friction it adds 10% pressure, and if they are stronger it removes 10%. This is a simple bang-bang rule.
  5. Brake assembly and vehicle. Pressure becomes clamping force through the disc brake geometry, four wheels apply it, and deceleration is a = Fb / m.

Equations and parameters

pc = K · l1 / lp   (K = 1×106 Pa)
pb = pc sin θ  (nonlinear)  |  pb ≈ pc θ  (linearized)
Ff = m g μ(t)     Fb = 4 · pb · Fb,c
Fb,c = [ μpr π ds2 / (3 rt) ] · (rro3 − rri3) / (rro2 − rri2)
Ffb = Ff − Fb     a = Fb / m

    p_b = 1.10 .* p_b;   % brakes weaker than friction: add pressure
elseif F_fb < -0.0001
    p_b = 0.90 .* p_b;   % brakes stronger than friction: release
end

F_fb = F_f - F_b;             % feedback error

Inputs

Friction is drawn at random every time the script runs (0.8 to 0.9 on pavement, 0.2 to 0.4 on ice), so each run is a slightly different road.

  • With the ABS rule, deceleration follows the road: about 8 m/s² on pavement (friction near 0.85) and about 2.9 m/s² on ice (friction near 0.3), which is what friction times gravity predicts.
  • The linearized model is within about 1% because sin θ is almost equal to θ at 15 degrees.
  • Peaks near 9.6 m/s² appear when the 10% pressure steps overshoot the traction limit.

Limits and what I would add next

Honest limits: this is a force-balance model, not a full wheel-dynamics model. It does not compute wheel speed or slip ratio, the cosine case multiplies its output by a gain of 20 to scale it, and the random friction changes every run.
  • Add wheel rotation and slip ratio so the ABS can act on slip directly.
  • Move the model to Simulink and sweep the 10% pressure step size.
  • Fix the random friction seed so runs can be compared exactly.
